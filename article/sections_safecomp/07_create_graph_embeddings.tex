\section{Graph Embeddings for Traffic Scene Analysis}
\label{chapter:implementation_of_graph_embeddings_for_traffic_scene_analysis}

Real-world traffic scene graphs exhibit significantly higher complexity than the predefined archetypes of Section~\ref{chapter:create_subgraphs_for_coverage_analysis}, motivating a complementary top-down approach based on graph embeddings.

Embeddings translate raw data into a vector space where distances measure similarity.
For traffic scene graphs, embeddings enable coverage analysis by comparing scenes: identifying similar simulation--real-world scenario pairs, detecting near duplicates, or visualizing structures that would be intractable in the original space of all possible traffic scenes.

A Graph Isomorphism Network with Edge features (GINE) \cite{hu2020strategiespretraininggraphneural} is used to generate embeddings, implemented in PyG \cite{Fey/Lenssen/2019}.
GINE is chosen as it allows embedding of edge features, so we may encode actor relation type and distance as integral descriptors for a traffic scene (see Figure~\ref{fig:graph_gine_architecture}); attention-based architectures such as graph attention networks~\cite{velickovic2018gat} are a common alternative but do not natively encode edge attributes in their original formulation.


\begin{figure}[tb]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{plots/graph_gine_architecture_tikz.pdf}
    \caption{Architecture of the GINE-based graph embedding model and its self-supervised training.
    A traffic scene graph $G$ carries $7$-dimensional node features (speed, actor type, lane-change and intersection flags) and $7$-dimensional edge features (path length and one-hot edge type).
    Two stochastically augmented views (Gaussian noise $\sigma=0.08$, edge dropout $p=0.1$) are passed through a weight-shared encoder $f_\theta$ of five GINE layers ($d=384$), each performing edge-conditioned message passing followed by batch normalisation, ReLU and dropout.
    Node states are aggregated by concatenated mean/max/sum pooling ($\mathbb{R}^{1152}$), mapped by an $\ell_2$-normalised MLP to the scene embedding $\mathbf{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{192}$ used downstream, and by a projection head $g_\psi$ to the space where the NT-Xent/InfoNCE contrastive loss ($\tau=0.07$) is computed between the two views.}
    \label{fig:graph_gine_architecture}
\end{figure}

The node and edge features and the full architecture are summarised in Figure~\ref{fig:graph_gine_architecture}. The model was trained on CARLA and Argoverse 2.0 using self-supervised contrastive learning \cite{chen2020simple,hu2020strategiespretraininggraphneural}: for each mini-batch two augmented views are created, and the contrastive loss maximises their agreement while contrasting against the other in-batch graphs. Optimization used AdamW (weight decay $5 \times 10^{-6}$), learning-rate warmup over three epochs, and exponential decay (initial rate 0.0015, factor 0.85) across 15 training stages \cite{Fey/Lenssen/2019}.
